# Pennsylvania First 20 Days — Initial Response

## Answer deadline — 20 days

Pennsylvania defendants have **20 days** to respond to a
complaint (Pa. R.C.P. 1026(a)).

**Extensions**: by agreement of the parties (10-day
extensions routinely granted by stipulation); or by
application to the court.

## Preliminary Objections (Pa. R.C.P. 1028)

Preliminary objections are the Pennsylvania equivalent of
a motion to dismiss. Grounds include:
- Lack of jurisdiction over the subject matter or person
- Improper venue
- Insufficient pleading — **demurrer** (failure to state
  a cause of action) — this is PA's equivalent of a
  12(b)(6) motion
- Insufficient process
- Pendency of a prior action
- Non-joinder of a necessary party
- Statute of frauds
- Statute of limitations apparent on the face of the
  complaint

After preliminary objections are filed, the non-moving
party has 20 days to file a response. The court then
rules (often without argument unless requested).

## Affirmative Defenses — New Matter (Pa. R.C.P. 1030)

In Pennsylvania, affirmative defenses must be raised in
a **New Matter** section of the Answer (Pa. R.C.P. 1030).
New Matter must:
- Be set forth in numbered paragraphs
- Each affirmative defense in a separate paragraph
- Include all facts supporting the defense

Common affirmative defenses in Pennsylvania:
- Statute of limitations (42 Pa. C.S. §§ 5524-5527)
- Payment/accord and satisfaction
- Contributory negligence / comparative fault
- Assumption of risk
- Res judicata / collateral estoppel
- Standing
- FDCPA / UTPCPL violations (consumer debt cases)

## Counterclaims (Pa. R.C.P. 1031)

Pennsylvania counterclaims may be **compulsory** or
**permissive**. A counterclaim arising from the same
transaction must be asserted or it may be barred
(Pa. R.C.P. 1031(a)).
