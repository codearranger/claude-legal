# Pennsylvania Post-Judgment

## Pennsylvania's dramatic wage garnishment exemption

Pennsylvania is one of the few states that **prohibits
wage garnishment for most consumer debts** (42 Pa. C.S.
§ 8127). Wages are exempt from execution except for:
- Child support and spousal support
- Board for four weeks or less
- Rent (limited)
- Federal taxes
- Student loans (federal)

This means a judgment creditor **cannot garnish the
debtor's wages** for credit card debt, medical bills,
personal loans, or similar consumer debts.

## Property exemptions (42 Pa. C.S. §§ 8121-8127)

| Exemption | Amount |
|---|---|
| Personal property exemption | $300 per judgment debtor (§ 8123) — very low |
| Wearing apparel | Fully exempt |
| Bibles and school books | Fully exempt |
| Sewing machine (family use) | Fully exempt |
| Uniform and arms of militia member | Fully exempt |

**Note**: Pennsylvania has no homestead exemption for
general creditors (unlike Florida or Texas). The debtor's
home is subject to execution by judgment creditors.

## Execution procedure (Pa. R.C.P. 3100 et seq.)

1. File **Praecipe for Writ of Execution** with the
   Prothonotary (clerk of CCP)
2. Sheriff receives the writ and levies on non-exempt
   property
3. **Bank account levy**: sheriff serves the financial
   institution; debtor has 30 days to claim exemptions
4. **Real property levy**: judgment lien filed + sheriff
   sale after proper notice

## Judgment lien (42 Pa. C.S. § 4303)

A judgment entered in the CCP creates a **lien on all
real property** of the judgment debtor in that county
from the date of entry. To reach property in other
counties, the judgment must be transferred to those
counties (certified copy filed with each prothonotary).

## Petition to Open or Strike Judgment

- **Strike**: judgment void on its face (defective process,
  wrong court, etc.); available at any time
- **Open**: judgment entered by default; equitable remedy;
  must show: (1) prompt action; (2) meritorious defense;
  (3) excuse for not responding
