# Pennsylvania Personal Injury — Subject-Matter Bundle

> **NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** Pennsylvania's medical malpractice
> certificate-of-merit requirement (Pa. R.C.P. 1042.3) is
> strictly enforced — failure results in dismissal. MCARE
> Act (40 Pa. C.S. § 1301) governs med-mal procedure.
> Government sovereign immunity has only 9 narrow exceptions.
> Verify all SOLs and procedural prerequisites before filing.

## Comparative fault — § 7102

Pennsylvania uses **modified comparative fault (51% bar)**
(42 Pa. C.S. § 7102):
- Plaintiff recovers if plaintiff's fault is **50% or less**
- At 51% or more fault: no recovery
- Each defendant pays in proportion to their fault
  (several liability), EXCEPT: when the defendant's fault
  is 60% or greater, joint-and-several liability applies
  (§ 7102(a.1))

## Medical malpractice — MCARE Act + Pa. R.C.P. 1042.3

### Certificate of Merit (Pa. R.C.P. 1042.3)

Before or within **60 days** of filing a malpractice complaint,
plaintiff must file a **Certificate of Merit** signed by
plaintiff's attorney (or the plaintiff pro se) stating that
a licensed professional has concluded that there is a
reasonable probability that the defendant deviated from an
acceptable professional standard and that this deviation was
the cause of the plaintiff's injuries.

**Failure to file** the certificate results in dismissal
(Pa. R.C.P. 1042.6 — upon praecipe by defendant).

### SOL

**2 years** from the date when the injury was known or should
have been known (42 Pa. C.S. § 5524(2)). Minor exception: SOL
tolled until age 18 for minor plaintiffs.

### MCARE Act (40 Pa. C.S. § 1301)

MCARE governs med-mal insurance and certain procedural
aspects (mandatory offer of nonbinding mediation; periodic
payment of future damages; limitations on expert qualifications).

## Product liability — *Tincher v. Omega Flex, Inc.*

*Tincher v. Omega Flex, Inc.*, 628 Pa. 521 (2014) rejected
the *Azzarello* strict liability standard and adopted the
**Restatement (Second) Torts § 402A** consumer-expectation
and risk-utility tests:

1. **Consumer-expectation test**: the product failed to
   perform as a reasonable consumer would expect
2. **Risk-utility test**: the risks of the product outweigh
   its benefits

The plaintiff may choose either theory; the defendant may
defend against both.

## Government sovereign immunity (42 Pa. C.S. § 8521 et seq.)

Pennsylvania government entities have sovereign immunity for
tort claims **except** for 9 statutory exceptions (§ 8522(b)):

1. Vehicle liability (government vehicles)
2. Medical-professional liability
3. Care, custody, and control of personal property
4. Commonwealth real estate, highways, and sidewalks
5. Potholes and other dangerous conditions of highways
6. Care, custody, and control of animals
7. Liquor store sales (for Commonwealth)
8. National Guard activities
9. Toxoids and vaccines

Claims not within these 9 exceptions remain barred by
sovereign immunity.

**Notice requirement**: file a written notice of claim with
the government entity before suit. Time limits vary by entity —
verify the specific entity's claims procedures.
