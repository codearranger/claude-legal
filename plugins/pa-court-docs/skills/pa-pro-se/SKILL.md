# Pennsylvania Pro Se — Civil Framework

> **NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** Pennsylvania's civil courts hold
> self-represented litigants to the same procedural rules
> as attorneys. The verification requirement (Pa. R.C.P. 1024)
> is particularly important — unsigned or unverified pleadings
> can be stricken. Consult the Pennsylvania Bar Association's
> Lawyer Referral Service for an initial consultation.

## Pro se resources

- **Pennsylvania Bar Association Lawyer Referral Service**:

## Verification requirement

Pennsylvania pleadings typically require a **verification**
(Pa. R.C.P. 1024) sworn under penalty of 18 Pa. C.S. § 4904.
This is a significant difference from other states where
unsworn declarations are common.

## MDJ — simplest starting point for small claims

For civil claims ≤ $12,000, the MDJ tier offers:
- Simplified procedures (Pa. R.C.P.M.D.J.)
- No requirement for formal pleadings
- Informal hearing before the MDJ
- Filing in person at the local MDJ office

## Common Pennsylvania pro se mistakes

1. **Missing verification**: pleadings must be verified;
   failure results in a motion to strike
2. **Wrong court**: MDJ (≤ $12k) vs. CCP (> $12k); filing
   in the wrong tier requires transfer and delays
3. **Missing certificate of service**: every filing must
   include proof of service on the opposing party
4. **Wrong county**: venue rules require filing in the
   defendant's county of residence or where the claim arose
