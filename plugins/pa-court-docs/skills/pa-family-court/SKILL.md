# Pennsylvania Family Court

## At a glance — Pennsylvania family law

- **No-fault divorce**: available after 2-year separation
  (consent of both parties — § 3301(c)) or after 2 years
  of separation (§ 3301(d) — no consent needed)
- **Fault-based divorce**: available on enumerated grounds
  (§ 3301(a)) — rarely used
- **Equitable distribution**: Pennsylvania is an equitable-
  distribution state (§ 3502); marital property defined broadly
  (§ 3501)
- **Residency**: 6 months in Pennsylvania (§ 3104(b))
- **Forum**: Court of Common Pleas, Family Division (or Domestic
  Relations Court for support matters)

## Divorce procedure (Pa. R.C.P. 1920 et seq.)

1. File **Complaint in Divorce** (Pa. R.C.P. 1920.12)
2. Serve defendant; 20-day response period
3. Spouse may file a Counterclaim
4. **Economic claims** (equitable distribution, alimony,
   attorney's fees) must be raised early or may be waived
5. Resolution by agreement (§ 3101 agreement) or at
   a hearing before a Master in Divorce (county-appointed
   domestic relations hearing officer)
6. Final Decree in Divorce entered by the court

## Child custody (23 Pa. C.S. §§ 5321-5340)

Pennsylvania has a codified **best-interests-of-the-child**
framework with 16 statutory factors (§ 5328). Custody types:
- **Physical custody**: primary, partial, shared, supervised
- **Legal custody**: sole or shared

Parenting plan (custody order) required. Courts strongly
consider **primary caretaker**, sibling relationships, and
history of abuse.

**Relocation**: parent seeking to relocate with the child
must comply with the strict notice and court-approval
requirements of § 5337.

## Child support (Pa. R.C.P. 1910 et seq.)

Pennsylvania uses the **Income Shares Model** guidelines
published by the Pennsylvania Support Guidelines. Both
parents' net monthly incomes, custody schedule, health
insurance, and child-care costs are factored.

Support orders are issued by Domestic Relations Sections
(county-level) and enforced by income withholding.

## Protection From Abuse (PFA) — 23 Pa. C.S. § 6101 et seq.

PFA orders protect victims of domestic violence, sexual
violence, or stalking.

**Ex parte temporary PFA**: issued on the day of filing if
the petition shows immediate danger. Lasts until the
hearing date.

**Hearing**: within **10 business days** of the ex parte
order. If the court finds abuse, a final PFA is entered
for up to **3 years** (renewable).

**Criminal contempt** for PFA violation: up to 6 months
imprisonment and $1,000 fine for first violation.
