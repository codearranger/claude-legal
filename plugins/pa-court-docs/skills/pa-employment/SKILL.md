# Pennsylvania Employment Law — Subject-Matter Bundle

> **NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** Pennsylvania's PHRA requires PHRC
> administrative exhaustion before filing suit. PHRA filing
> deadline is 180 days (shorter than EEOC's 300-day period
> in PA). WPCL provides powerful penalty wages for willful
> non-payment. Verify all deadlines and procedures before
> filing.

## PHRA (Pennsylvania Human Relations Act) — 43 Pa. C.S. § 951

### Coverage

PHRA applies to employers with **4+ employees** (broader
coverage than federal Title VII's 15+ threshold). Protected
characteristics: race, color, familial status, religious creed,
ancestry, age (40-70 for employers), sex, national origin,
disability, and use of a guide dog.

**Note**: Pennsylvania's PHRA does not expressly cover sexual
orientation or gender identity — check for local ordinances
(Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, Harrisburg have broader protections)
and federal EEOC/Title VII coverage (post-*Bostock v. Clayton
County*).

### PHRC administrative exhaustion

**Mandatory** before filing a civil action:
1. File PHRC charge within **180 days** of the discriminatory
   act (43 Pa. C.S. § 959(h))
2. Wait for PHRC investigation (minimum 1 year unless PHRC
   acts sooner)
3. After 1 year, claimant may request to bring a civil action
   or wait for PHRC to issue a right-to-sue

**EEOC dual-filing**: PHRC and EEOC have a worksharing
agreement. A charge filed with PHRC is cross-filed with EEOC.
Federal deadline in Pennsylvania: 300 days.

### Damages

PHRA does not cap damages. Available: back pay, front pay,
emotional distress, reinstatement, attorney's fees.

## WPCL (Wage Payment and Collection Law) — 43 Pa. C.S. § 260.1

The WPCL provides:
- Employer must pay all wages on the established payday
- **Penalty wages** for non-payment: **25% of unpaid wages**
  or $500, whichever is greater, if the employer **willfully
  or negligently** fails to pay (§ 260.10)
- **Liquidated damages** also available in some situations
- Attorney's fees to the prevailing employee (§ 260.9a(f))
- **SOL**: 3 years from the date wages were due (§ 260.9a(g))

## Non-competes — Pennsylvania common law

Pennsylvania has not enacted a non-compete statute. Courts
apply common law. A non-compete is enforceable only if:
1. It is ancillary to a lawful employment relationship
2. Supported by adequate consideration
3. Reasonably limited in **time, geographic scope, and type
   of activity** restricted
4. Not greater than reasonably necessary to protect the
   employer's legitimate interests

*Hess v. Gebhard & Co.*, 570 Pa. 148 (2002): courts should
enforce non-competes narrowly and are reluctant to blue-pencil
overbroad agreements in some jurisdictions.

**Note**: a non-compete entered at the start of employment
is supported by the job itself as consideration. Mid-employment
non-competes require additional consideration.
