# California Family Law — Subject-Matter Bundle

> **NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** California family law is governed by the Family
> Code (over 5,000 sections) and complex judicial procedures. The
> DVRO protective-order process is sensitive and time-critical; contact
> a domestic-violence hotline or legal-aid family-law unit immediately.
> Child support figures change with income; spousal support turns on
> case-specific factors. Verify all procedural steps and statutory
> cites against current Family Code and local rules before filing.

## At a glance — California family law fundamentals

- **Forum**: Superior Court, Family Law Department (most counties have
  a dedicated family law department with its own local rules)
- **Community property**: California is a community-property state.
  All assets acquired during marriage are presumed community property
  (Fam. Code § 760); separate property is what was owned before
  marriage or received by gift/inheritance during marriage (§ 770)
- **No-fault divorce only**: irreconcilable differences (Fam. Code
  § 2310(a)) or incurable insanity. No fault-based grounds.
- **Residency**: at least one party must have been a resident of
  California for **6 months** and of the county where filed for
  **3 months** before filing (Fam. Code § 2320)
- **6-month waiting period**: earliest a dissolution can be final is
  **6 months and 1 day** from service of the summons on respondent
  (Fam. Code § 2339)

## Divorce (dissolution of marriage) procedure

### Standard FL forms

California family law is heavily forms-driven. The Judicial Council
publishes mandatory FL-series forms:

| Form | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| FL-100 | Petition for Dissolution / Legal Separation / Nullity |
| FL-110 | Summons (Family Law) |
| FL-130 | Appearance, Stipulations, and Waivers |
| FL-140 | Declaration of Disclosure — Schedule of Assets and Debts |
| FL-150 | Income and Expense Declaration |
| FL-160 | Property Declaration |
| FL-170 | Declaration for Default or Uncontested Dissolution |
| FL-180 | Judgment (for the court to sign) |

**Service**: respondent must be personally served with FL-100 + FL-110
and supporting papers (CCP § 415.10 or substituted service under
§ 415.20). Respondent has **30 days** to file a Response (FL-120).

**Preliminary Declarations of Disclosure** are mandatory and must be
served (not filed) on the other party early in the proceeding
(Fam. Code §§ 2102-2105).

## Community property — Fam. Code §§ 760-853

### The general rule

All property acquired by either spouse during marriage while domiciled
in California is community property (§ 760). Each spouse owns an
**undivided one-half interest** in community property.

### Separate property

- Property owned before marriage
- Property acquired during marriage by gift, bequest, devise, or descent
- Rents, profits, and income from separate property (§ 770)
- Property acquired after permanent separation

### Equal division rule

At dissolution, the court must divide community property **equally**
(Fam. Code § 2550) unless the parties agree otherwise. Exceptions:
misappropriation of community assets, deliberate prevention of fair
division (§ 2602).

### Characterization issues

Tracing from separate property to commingled funds; transmutation
(§ 852 — requires written agreement signed by adversely affected
spouse); Moore/Marsden formula for real property purchased partly
before marriage.

### Retirement accounts and pensions

Defined-benefit pension earned during marriage is community property.
Requires a **Qualified Domestic Relations Order (QDRO)** (or, for
public pensions, a Domestic Relations Order) to divide. QDRO must be
prepared separately and submitted to the plan administrator.

## Child custody — Fam. Code § 3011

### Best-interests factors (§ 3011)

The court determines custody based on the best interests of the child,
considering: health, safety, and welfare; history of abuse; nature and
amount of contact with each parent; habitual or continual illegal use of
controlled substances or alcohol; and the child's preference (if of
sufficient age and capacity — generally 14+ per § 3042).

### Legal vs. physical custody

- **Legal custody**: right to make decisions about education, health,
  religious upbringing. Joint legal custody is common.
- **Physical custody**: where the child lives. Joint physical custody
  or primary custody with one parent.

### UCCJEA (Fam. Code §§ 3400-3465)

California has adopted the UCCJEA. Home state (where child lived for
6 months before filing) has initial jurisdiction. Critical for
relocation and interstate disputes.

### Parenting plans

Courts strongly prefer parental agreements on parenting schedules.
Contested custody requires attendance at Family Court Services (CCRC
mediation) before a contested hearing.

## Child support — CA Child Support Guidelines (Fam. Code §§ 4050-4076)

### The DissoMaster / Guideline formula

California uses the **algebraic guideline formula** (§ 4055):
CS = K [HN(1 – H) – N]

The variables are: parents' net monthly disposable incomes, the
timeshare percentage for each parent. The court must follow the
guideline amount unless rebuttal factors apply (§ 4057).

**California Child Support Services**: county departments of child
support services (DCSS) can enforce support and establish orders
administratively.

### Duration

Support continues until the child turns **18** (or 19 if still in
high school full time and living with a parent — § 3901). May be
modified on showing of changed circumstances.

## Spousal support (alimony) — Fam. Code § 4320

At permanent dissolution, the court considers the § 4320 factors:
marketable skills; extent spouse helped other's career; supporting
spouse's contribution to education; ability of supporting spouse to
pay; needs based on standard of living; obligations and assets of each;
duration of marriage; custodial responsibility; domestic violence
history; age and health; tax consequences; hardship; balance of
hardships.

**General rule of thumb** (not a rule): for marriages under 10 years,
support for roughly half the length of the marriage; for marriages of
10+ years (a "long-term marriage"), support potentially indefinite with
periodic review.

**Temporary support** (pendente lite): per the DissoMaster formula;
different from the § 4320 permanent-support standard.

## Domestic Violence Restraining Orders (DVRO)

### Emergency Protective Order (EPO)

Law enforcement can issue an EPO (§ 646.91 Pen. Code) on-scene that
lasts 5-7 days. The court then issues a TRO.

### Temporary Restraining Order (TRO)

Petitioner files FL-300 (Request for DVRO) + FL-301 (Notice of
Court Hearing) + supporting FL-303 declaration. Court can issue
ex parte TRO **same day** (§ 6320). Lasts until the noticed hearing
(typically 20-25 days).

### Permanent DVRO

At the noticed hearing, if the court finds abuse (§ 6300 — reasonable
apprehension of imminent serious bodily injury or infliction of
physical injury), the court can issue a DVRO for **up to 5 years**
(§ 6345). Renewable indefinitely.

**Firearms**: a DVRO requires surrender/transfer of all firearms
within 24 hours (§ 6389; federal 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(8) also applies).
