# Oregon Family Law — Subject-Matter Bundle

> **NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** Oregon family-law statutes change regularly —
> child-support guidelines are administratively updated, spousal-support
> caselaw evolves, and domestic-violence protective-order rules are
> sensitive and jurisdiction-specific. Verify every procedural step and
> statutory cite against current ORS and OAR before relying. For
> domestic-violence matters, contact a local domestic-violence advocacy
> program or Oregon Law Center immediately.

## At a glance

- **Primary statutes**: ORS Chapter 107 (dissolution, separation,
  annulment, spousal support, FAPA orders); ORS Chapter 108 (marital
  rights); ORS Chapter 109 (Uniform Parentage Act — paternity, custody,
  adoption); ORS Chapter 25 (child support enforcement + Guidelines)
- **Governing rules**: UTCR 2.010 statewide format; UTCR Supplement
  section 8 (family-law-specific); Oregon Family Abuse Prevention Act
  (FAPA) ORS 107.700-107.735
- **Division of property**: equitable distribution (not community
  property) — judge has broad discretion; presumption of equal division
  does not apply to separate property
- **Forum**: Circuit Court (Family Law Department in Multnomah and other
  large counties; general civil calendar elsewhere)

## Dissolution of marriage (divorce)

### Grounds and residency

Oregon is a **no-fault-only** state. The sole ground is
**irreconcilable differences** (ORS 107.025). No fault-based grounds exist.

Residency requirement: at least one party must be a resident of Oregon
for **6 months** before filing (ORS 107.075(1)). For members of the
armed forces stationed in Oregon, the 6-month residency requirement
applies differently — see ORS 107.075(2).

### Procedure

1. **Petition for Dissolution of Marriage** (SOU-UD-004 or equivalent)
2. **Summons** (SOU-UD-001 / UTCR-approved form) — 30-day response time
3. **Temporary orders** if needed (support, custody, use of residence,
   injunction against dissipation of assets)
4. **Service** on respondent (ORCP 7; personal service or mail if waived)
5. **Response** (30 days from service, ORCP 7; or Appearance/Waiver)
6. **Judgment of Dissolution** incorporating parenting plan + support +
   property division

**Default judgment** available if respondent does not appear (UTCR 8.010).
For uncontested cases, the petitioner submits a Decree of Dissolution and
Supporting Affidavit without a hearing in many counties.

## Child custody — legal and physical

Oregon uses a two-part framework:
- **Legal custody** — decision-making authority (ORS 107.169)
- **Physical custody / parenting time** — where the child lives; the
  court-approved schedule

Joint legal custody is possible only by **agreement of both parties**
(ORS 107.169(3)) — the court cannot impose joint legal custody over one
parent's objection.

**Best-interests factors** (ORS 107.137): the court considers the
emotional ties between the child and each parent; each parent's interest
and attitude; the desirability of continuing an existing relationship;
the abuse history of either parent; the preference of the primary
caregiver; the willingness and ability of each parent to facilitate a
close and continuing relationship with the other parent.

**Parenting plan**: ORS 107.102 requires every custody order to include
a parenting plan specifying the parenting-time schedule, holiday and
vacation schedule, and a dispute-resolution process.

## Child support — Oregon Child Support Guidelines

Oregon uses **income-shares model** guidelines at ORS 25.275 and OAR
Chapter 137 (DOJ guidelines). The formula combines both parents' gross
incomes, adjusts for parenting time, health insurance, work-related child
care, and extraordinary expenses. Oregon's **child-support calculator**
is maintained by the Oregon DOJ Child Support Program at
`oregonchildsupport.gov`.

Key points:
- Support runs until the child turns **18**, or through the school year
  in which the child turns 18, whichever is later (ORS 107.108); may
  extend to age 21 if child is a full-time student
- **Modification** requires a substantial change in circumstances (ORS
  107.135); automatic administrative adjustment available every 3 years
  through the DOJ program
- **Income withholding order** is standard and issued automatically

## Spousal support (alimony)

ORS 107.105(1)(d) authorizes three types of spousal support:

| Type | Purpose | Typical duration |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| **Transitional** | Funds education or training for self-sufficiency | Time-limited |
| **Compensatory** | Compensates for contribution to other party's education/career | Time-limited |
| **Maintenance** | For a spouse unable to achieve self-support | May be indefinite |

Courts consider duration of marriage, health, standard of living,
relative earning capacities, child custody, tax consequences, fault
(relevant to maintenance support, ORS 107.105(1)(f)), and other
equitable factors.

## Property division — equitable distribution

Oregon is an **equitable distribution** state (ORS 107.105(1)(f)).
The court divides all property acquired during the marriage (and
sometimes separate property brought in, depending on commingling),
**equitably** — which may or may not be equal.

**Separate property** (acquired before marriage or by gift/inheritance
during marriage) generally remains separate, but commingling or
transmutation can change its character.

**The marital estate** includes retirement accounts (QDRO process for
401(k)/pension), stock options, business interests, and equity in real
property.

## FAPA domestic-violence protective orders (ORS 107.700-107.735)

The **Family Abuse Prevention Act (FAPA)** provides a civil protective
order remedy for abuse within the family or household.

### Ex parte temporary order

A petitioner may obtain a **temporary protective order ex parte**
(without notice to the respondent) by showing **abuse** (ORS 107.700(1)
physical abuse, attempted/threatened physical abuse, or causing the
petitioner fear of imminent serious physical injury) committed within the
preceding **180 days**.

The temporary order can: prohibit contact and proximity; require
respondent to move from a shared residence; award temporary custody of
children.

### Contested hearing

After the ex parte order, the respondent has a right to a hearing
(**within 21 days** of requesting one — ORS 107.718(9)). The petitioner
bears the burden at the hearing. If the protective order is continued,
it runs for **1 year** (renewable).

**Firearms**: a FAPA order automatically triggers the federal firearm-
prohibition under 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(8) and requires surrender of
firearms.

## Paternity / parentage (ORS Chapter 109)

Oregon has adopted a version of the **Uniform Parentage Act** (2000 UPA).
Parentage can be established by:
- **Voluntary Acknowledgment of Paternity (VAP)** — administrative;
  signed at hospital or DHS
- **Administrative establishment** through the DOJ Child Support Program
- **Judicial action** — Complaint to Establish Paternity (ORS 109.125)

DNA genetic testing is available on request; results creating a
statutory presumption (ORS 109.070).
