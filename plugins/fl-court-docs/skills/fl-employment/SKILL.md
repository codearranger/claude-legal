# Florida Employment Law — Subject-Matter Bundle

> **NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** Florida employment law differs from most
> states in being relatively employer-favorable. Florida enforces
> non-competes (§ 542.335); the FCHR administrative-exhaustion
> deadline is short; and Florida has no state-level paid sick
> leave mandate. Verify all deadlines and procedures against
> current Florida Statutes before filing.

## At a glance

- **Anti-discrimination**: Florida Civil Rights Act (FCRA,
  § 760.01-760.11); enforcement by Florida Commission on Human
  Relations (FCHR)
- **Wage/hour**: Florida Constitution Art. X § 24 (minimum wage);
  § 448 (wage payment); no state overtime law (federal FLSA applies)
- **Non-competes**: § 542.335 — Florida enforces non-competes if
  they protect a legitimate business interest; presumptively valid
  if they meet the statutory requirements
- **Workers' comp**: § 440 — exclusive remedy; workers' comp
  immunity applies broadly

## Florida Civil Rights Act (FCRA) — § 760

### Coverage

FCRA applies to employers with **15+ employees** (same as federal
Title VII). Protected characteristics: race, color, religion, sex,
national origin, age (40+), handicap (disability), marital status,
and pregnancy.

Florida has **no sexual-orientation or gender-identity
protected class** in the statute (pending legislative change;
some local ordinances fill this gap in Miami-Dade, Broward,
Orlando, and other localities).

### FCHR administrative exhaustion

Before filing a civil action, claimant must file a **FCHR
charge within 365 days** (1 year) of the unlawful employment
practice (§ 760.11(1)).

After 180 days (or earlier upon request), the FCHR may issue
a **Notice of Right to Sue**. The civil suit must be filed
within **1 year** of the right-to-sue notice (§ 760.11(4)(b)).

**EEOC dual-filing**: Florida is a "deferral state" — EEOC
will cross-file an FCHR charge, and vice versa. Federal
Title VII deadline is 300 days in Florida.

## Non-competes (§ 542.335)

Unlike most states, Florida **enforces** non-competes and
non-solicitation agreements that meet the statutory requirements:

- Must be in writing, signed by the employee
- Must protect a **legitimate business interest**: trade secrets,
  substantial relationships with customers, extraordinary or
  specialized training, valuable confidential information
- **Presumptively valid** enforcement periods: 6 months for
  non-solicitation of customers/employees; 2 years for non-
  competition with a former employer; 3 years protecting trade
  secrets

Florida courts are directed to **enforce the agreement to the
extent reasonable** — a "blue-pencil" approach is available.
Geographic and time restrictions that are overbroad can be reduced
rather than voiding the entire agreement.

**Injunctive relief** is the primary remedy; irreparable harm
is presumed from a breach of a valid non-compete (§ 542.335(1)(j)).

## Florida minimum wage (Art. X § 24)

Florida's minimum wage is in the Florida Constitution (Art. X § 24),
indexed annually to the CPI. Florida's minimum wage exceeds the
federal minimum. Verify the current rate at the Florida Department
of Economic Opportunity (DEO) website.

**Amendment 2 (2020)**: scheduled gradual increases to $15/hr
by September 2026. Verify the current rate.

## Workers' compensation — § 440

Florida workers' comp provides the **exclusive remedy** for
workplace injuries (§ 440.11). Employer immunity is broad.
Exceptions:
- Employer **intentional tort**: requires proof that the
  employer engaged in conduct virtually certain to result
  in injury (very narrow — *Eller v. Showa Denko K.K.*)
- **Dual-capacity doctrine** (very limited in Florida)

**OSHA violations**: do not automatically create a tort action
in Florida.
