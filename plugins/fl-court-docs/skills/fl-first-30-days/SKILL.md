# Florida First 30 Days — Initial Response

## Answer deadline

Florida defendants have **20 days** to respond to a complaint
after service (Fla. R. Civ. P. 1.140(a)). This is shorter
than the federal 21-day period.

**Extensions**: Florida courts routinely grant 20-day
extensions on stipulation; file a Notice of Extension or
Motion for Extension before the deadline.

## Motion to Dismiss (Rule 1.140(b))

Grounds for MTD before answering:
- Lack of jurisdiction over the subject matter
- Lack of jurisdiction over the person
- Improper venue
- Insufficiency of process or service of process
- Failure to state a cause of action
- Failure to join indispensable party

An MTD **tolls** the time to answer; defendant has 10 days
after the court rules on the MTD to file an answer if the
MTD is denied.

## Florida Fact-Pleading Standard

Florida uses **fact pleading** (not Twombly/Iqbal notice
pleading). A complaint must state the ultimate facts supporting
each element of each cause of action. Conclusory allegations
are insufficient. This matters both for drafting complaints
and for attacking deficient ones.

## Affirmative Defenses

Florida Rule 1.110(d) lists affirmative defenses that must
be raised in the answer (or they are waived). Key defenses
in civil cases:
- Statute of limitations (§ 95.11)
- Estoppel, waiver, laches
- Payment / accord and satisfaction
- Statute of frauds
- Res judicata / collateral estoppel
- Lack of standing
- FDCPA / FCCPA violations (consumer debt)

## Compulsory Counterclaims (Rule 1.170(a))

A counterclaim is **compulsory** if it arises out of the same
transaction or occurrence as the plaintiff's claim and does not
require the presence of third parties that the court cannot
acquire jurisdiction over. Failure to assert a compulsory
counterclaim waives it.
