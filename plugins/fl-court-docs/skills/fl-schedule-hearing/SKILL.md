# Florida — Schedule a Hearing

## Step 1: Identify the JA

After case assignment, the JA (judicial assistant) for the
assigned judge is the primary contact for scheduling. JA
contact information is on the court's website by judge.

## Step 2: Determine hearing type

| Hearing type | Procedure |
|---|---|
| **Uniform Motion Calendar (UMC)** (≤ 15 min) | Self-schedule by calling or emailing the JA; some courts use an online scheduling system |
| **Regular hearing** (> 15 min) | Contact JA to obtain an available hearing slot; confirm with opposing counsel before finalizing |
| **Emergency / ex parte** | File motion for emergency hearing; contact JA immediately; must show genuine emergency |

## Step 3: File and serve Notice of Hearing

File the **Notice of Hearing** through PACE before the hearing.
The notice must state:
- Hearing date, time, location (and Zoom info if applicable)
- Estimated duration
- Motion(s) being heard

Serve the Notice of Hearing on all parties at least
**20 days** before the hearing date (Rule 1.090(e)), unless the
motion itself authorizes shorter notice.

## Step 4: Confirm with opposing counsel

Before finalizing the hearing date, **confirm** with opposing
counsel that they are available. Unilaterally setting a hearing
without checking availability is discouraged and may result
in a continuance.

## Step 5: Pre-hearing logistics

- Upload any exhibits or supporting materials to PACE in
  advance (check local procedure)
- Prepare a hearing binder with the motion, response, and key
  cases
- If Zoom: test the connection in advance; have a backup
  (phone dial-in)
