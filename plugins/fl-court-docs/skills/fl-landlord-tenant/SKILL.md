# Florida Landlord-Tenant — Subject-Matter Bundle

> **NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** Florida § 83 notices require exact compliance;
> a defective notice defeats the eviction action. Verify all notice
> periods, text requirements, and exemptions against current Florida
> Statutes before serving a notice or filing an eviction.

## At a glance

- **Primary statute**: Fla. Stat. §§ 83.40-83.683 (Residential
  Landlord and Tenant Act, Part II); §§ 83.001-83.251 (Non-residential,
  Part I); §§ 83.7 et seq. (Mobile home parks, Part III)
- **Eviction procedure**: Ch. 83, Part II + Fla. R. Civ. P. 1.125
  (residential eviction); Fla. Sm. Cl. Ct. R. 7.090 (for county court)
- **Forum**: County Court (civil jurisdiction ≤ $50,000; evictions of
  any rent amount)

## Notices — residential tenancies

| Situation | Notice required | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Nonpayment of rent | 3-day notice to pay or vacate | § 83.56(3) |
| Lease violation (curable) | 7-day notice to cure or vacate | § 83.56(2)(b) |
| Lease violation (incurable) | 7-day notice to vacate (no cure option) | § 83.56(2)(a) |
| Month-to-month tenancy termination (tenant) | 15-day notice | § 83.57(3) |
| Month-to-month tenancy termination (landlord) | 15-day notice | § 83.57(3) |
| Annual tenancy termination (60-day notice) | 60-day notice | § 83.575 |

**3-day notice requirements**: the 3-day notice must state the
amount of rent owed (no more, no less — mistakes void the notice)
and must give the tenant the option to pay or vacate.
Do not include late fees in the 3-day notice amount.

## Eviction procedure (Ch. 83 + Fla. R. Civ. P. 1.125)

1. Serve proper written notice (3-day / 7-day / 15-day as applicable)
2. Wait for the notice period to expire without cure or surrender
3. File **Complaint for Eviction** (and money damages if desired)
   in County Court
4. Pay filing fee (verify at clerk's website)
5. Process server serves summons + complaint on tenant
6. Tenant has **5 business days** to respond (§ 83.60(1)); **the
   response must include a deposit of the amount alleged unpaid
   into the court registry** — failure to deposit = default
7. If tenant fails to deposit: clerk issues **Writ of Possession**
   within 24 hours (§ 83.62(2))
8. Sheriff executes the writ (minimum 24-hour notice to tenant)

**Deposit requirement** (§ 83.60(2)): This is a distinctive
Florida rule — the tenant must pay the alleged rent into the
court registry when responding or filing a motion; failure
results in default for the landlord.

## Security deposits (§ 83.49)

- No statutory cap on the amount
- **15-day return deadline** if there are no deductions (§ 83.49(3)(a))
- **30-day return deadline** if there ARE deductions (§ 83.49(3)(b))
  — with written itemized statement
- Failure to timely return + comply: landlord **forfeits right to
  make deductions** and is liable for damages

## Habitability (§ 83.51)

Landlord must comply with building, housing, and health codes;
maintain structural components; maintain plumbing, heating/cooling,
and electrical systems; exterminate vermin.

Tenant remedy for breach: written notice + reasonable time to repair;
if landlord fails to repair within 7 days, tenant may terminate the
lease or seek damages.

## Retaliation (§ 83.64)

Landlord may not retaliate against tenant for complaining to a
government agency about habitability or code violations, or for
organizing a tenant association. Retaliatory actions within
1 year of protected activity are presumed retaliatory.
