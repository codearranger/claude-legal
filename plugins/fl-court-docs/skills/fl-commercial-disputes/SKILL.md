# Florida Commercial Disputes — Subject-Matter Bundle

> **NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** Florida commercial practice has unique
> features: fact-pleading standard; FDUTPA is available in B2B
> disputes; § 542.335 non-competes are enforceable; and the
> Proposal for Settlement rule (§ 768.79) creates significant
> fee-shifting risk. Verify all cites against current Florida
> Statutes.

## At a glance

- **Contract SOL**: 5 years for written contracts (§ 95.11(2)(b));
  4 years for oral contracts (§ 95.11(3)(k))
- **UCC**: Florida UCC codified at §§ 671-680 (Articles 1-9);
  4-year SOL for sales contracts (§ 672.725)
- **Business entities**: FL LLC Act (FRLLCA, § 605.0101 et seq.);
  FL Business Corporation Act (FBCA, § 607.0101 et seq.)
- **Arbitration**: Florida Arbitration Code (§ 682.01 et seq.)
  + Federal Arbitration Act (FAA)

## Breach of contract

### Florida fact-pleading requirement

Florida requires **fact pleading** — every element must be
supported by specific ultimate-fact allegations. Conclusory
allegations will not survive a motion to dismiss. This is
stricter than the federal Twombly/Iqbal notice-pleading standard.

### Attorney's fees

Florida follows the American Rule (each party bears own fees)
**unless** a statute or contract provides fees. Key fee provisions:
- **§ 57.105**: mandatory fees for frivolous claims or defenses
  (very actively used in Florida)
- **§ 768.79 (Proposal for Settlement)**: if a party serves a
  settlement offer that is rejected and the opposing party fails
  to obtain a result at least **25% more favorable**, the offeror
  is entitled to attorney's fees from the date of the offer
- **Contractual fee provisions** (§ 57.115): enforced

### Proposal for Settlement (§ 768.79) — fee-shifting risk

A party may serve a formal Offer of Judgment / Proposal for
Settlement. If the offeree rejects and fails to beat the offer
by 25%, the offeror is entitled to attorney's fees. This creates
significant strategic leverage in commercial cases.

## FDUTPA (§ 501.204) in commercial disputes

FDUTPA applies to B2B commercial disputes as well as consumer
transactions (subject to standing requirements). Available
remedies: actual damages; attorney's fees (§ 501.2105);
injunctive relief.

**Note**: unlike some states, Florida FDUTPA does not require
proof of a pattern of deceptive conduct — a single deceptive act
can suffice.

## Florida LLC Act (FRLLCA) — § 605.0101 et seq.

Florida adopted the FRLLCA effective January 1, 2015:
- **Fiduciary duties** of members/managers: § 605.04091
  (duty of loyalty + duty of care); operating agreement
  can restrict (§ 605.04092)
- **Judicial dissolution** (§ 605.0702): for member deadlock,
  waste, oppression; also available where the LLC has
  completed its activities
- **Derivative suits** (§ 605.0801): member may bring
  suit on behalf of the LLC

## Florida Business Corporation Act (FBCA) — § 607.0101

Key corporate-litigation provisions:
- **Shareholder inspection rights** (§ 607.1602-607.1605)
- **Derivative suits** (§ 607.07401): demand requirement
- **Judicial dissolution** (§ 607.1430-607.1435): deadlock,
  oppression, waste
- **Fiduciary duties**: directors bound by business-judgment
  rule (§ 607.0830)
- **Indemnification** (§ 607.0850-607.0858)

## Florida Arbitration Code (§ 682)

Florida has its own arbitration code in addition to the FAA.
Florida applies the FAA to contracts "involving commerce" (broad).
For domestic commercial arbitration, both may apply.

**Unconscionability** is a recognized defense to enforcement of
an arbitration clause (§ 682.03); Florida courts are less
aggressive than California in finding unconscionability.
