# Florida Discovery Framework

Florida discovery is governed by the **Florida Rules of Civil
Procedure, Rules 1.280-1.400**.

## Discovery tools and limits

| Tool | Rule | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Interrogatories | 1.340 | 30 per party (including subparts) without leave |
| Requests for Production | 1.350 | No numerical limit |
| Requests for Admission | 1.370 | No numerical limit |
| Depositions | 1.310 | 10 per side without leave; 7-hour day |
| Physical/Mental Exam | 1.360 | On order of court; good cause required |

## Key deadlines

- Respond to interrogatories and RFPs: **30 days** after service
  (Rule 1.340(a), 1.350(b))
- Respond to RFAs: **30 days** after service (Rule 1.370(a))
- Failure to respond to RFAs → admissions by operation of law

## E-discovery

Florida Rule 1.280(d) addresses electronically stored
information (ESI). Parties must discuss ESI preservation in
the Case Management Conference. Florida follows a
proportionality standard: discovery must be proportional to
the needs of the case.

## Motion to Compel

If a party fails to respond or objects improperly, file a
**Motion to Compel** (Rule 1.380). Must include a
certification of good-faith conferral. Serve 20 days'
notice (Rule 1.090(e) + standard motion notice).
