# Florida Personal Injury — Subject-Matter Bundle

> **NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** Florida's tort reform was significantly
> changed in 2023 by HB 837. The comparative fault standard was
> changed from pure comparative fault to **modified comparative
> fault (51% bar)**. HB 837 also made major changes to bad-faith
> claims, attorney's fees in negligence cases, and proposal for
> settlement rules. Verify current law before relying — pre-2023
> Florida PI precedent on comparative fault standard may not apply
> to cases filed after March 24, 2023.

## At a glance — HB 837 (2023) changes

Effective March 24, 2023, HB 837 made the following changes:
- **Modified comparative fault (51% bar)**: replaced pure
  comparative fault. Plaintiff at 51%+ fault = no recovery.
  Plaintiff at 50% or less = recovery reduced proportionally (§ 768.81)
- **Medical malpractice**: significant changes to NICA and
  pre-suit requirements
- **Attorney's fees in negligence**: limited fees for letters
  of protection; court must consider comparative fault in
  awarding fees
- **Proposal for Settlement**: changes to § 768.79 offer-of-
  judgment rule

## Negligence — § 768.81 (post-HB 837)

**Modified comparative fault (51% bar)** applies. Plaintiff
recovers if plaintiff's fault is **50% or less** of the
total fault. At 51%+ fault, plaintiff recovers nothing.

Multiple defendant liability: each defendant pays in proportion
to their percentage of fault (several liability); joint and
several liability is abolished except for certain categories.

## Government tort claims — sovereign immunity § 768.28

The State of Florida has waived sovereign immunity to the extent
provided in § 768.28. Key provisions:

- **$200,000 per person** / **$300,000 per incident** cap on
  state and local government liability (§ 768.28(5))
  — **verify current cap** (may be higher)
- **Notice prerequisite**: written notice to the agency and
  the Department of Financial Services within **3 years**
  of the event (§ 768.28(6)(a))
- Agency has **6 months** to act on the claim
- Only then may the claimant file suit (§ 768.28(6)(a))

## Medical malpractice — § 766 pre-suit screening

Before filing a medical-malpractice action, plaintiff must:
1. Conduct a **pre-suit investigation** (conduct and good faith
   basis — § 766.104)
2. Serve a **Notice of Intent to Initiate Litigation** on all
   defendants and their insurers (§ 766.106(2))
3. A **90-day pre-suit period** during which the parties
   engage in informal discovery (§ 766.106(3))
4. Defendant may offer settlement; if no offer or offer
   rejected, the pre-suit period ends

**SOL**: 2 years from discovery (§ 95.11(4)(b)); 4-year
statute of ultimate repose (§ 95.11(4)(b)(2)).

**NICA** (Florida Birth-Related Neurological Injury
Compensation Association): exclusive no-fault remedy for
qualifying birth-related neurological injuries.

## Wrongful death — § 768.16-768.26

Florida's Wrongful Death Act provides:
- Action by the **personal representative** of the estate
- Damages for: loss of support and services; loss of
  companionship, instruction, and guidance; mental pain and
  suffering of survivors; medical/funeral expenses
- SOL: **2 years** from date of death (§ 95.11(4)(d))
- **Survivors' claims**: each survivor's damages pled separately
