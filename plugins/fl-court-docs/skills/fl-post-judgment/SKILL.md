# Florida Post-Judgment

## Florida's significant exemptions

Florida has some of the most debtor-protective exemption laws
in the country.

### Homestead exemption (Florida Constitution Art. X § 4)

Florida's homestead exemption is **unlimited in value** for the
principal residence (with an acreage limit: ½ acre within a
municipality; 160 acres outside). This is constitutionally
protected — it cannot be modified by statute.

### Wage garnishment — 100% exempt for "head of family"

Under **Fla. Stat. § 222.11**, the wages, salary, and other
compensation of a person who **is the head of a family** residing
in Florida are **exempt from garnishment**. This is one of the
broadest wage-garnishment exemptions in the nation.

**Note**: a "head of family" is a person providing more than
50% of the support for a child or other dependent.

If the debtor is NOT the head of a family, only the federal
Consumer Credit Protection Act (CCPA) garnishment limits apply
(generally 25% of disposable earnings or the amount by which
disposable earnings exceed 30x federal minimum wage, whichever
is less).

### Personal property exemptions (§ 222)

- $1,000 personal property (§ 222.06) — can be applied to motor
  vehicle if no homestead exemption is claimed
- Additional $4,000 for head of family if no homestead (§ 222.25)
- Retirement accounts: fully exempt (§ 222.21)
- Health insurance cash value (§ 222.18)
- Life insurance cash value (§ 222.14)

## Writ of Execution / Garnishment Procedure

1. After entry of final judgment, file a **Motion for Writ of
   Execution** or **Motion for Writ of Garnishment** depending
   on the asset type
2. For **bank garnishment**: file an Application for Writ of
   Garnishment (§ 77.01); serve the writ on the financial
   institution
3. Debtor may claim exemptions in writing within **20 days**
   of service of the writ (§ 77.041)
4. If exemption is claimed, a hearing is set

## Motion to Vacate (Rule 1.540)

A judgment can be vacated for:
- Mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect
- Newly discovered evidence
- Fraud, misrepresentation, or misconduct
- Void judgment (e.g., improper service)
- Satisfaction, release, or prior judgment

**Time limits**: motions under grounds (a)-(c) must be filed
within **1 year** of the judgment. Motions for void judgments
have no time limit.
