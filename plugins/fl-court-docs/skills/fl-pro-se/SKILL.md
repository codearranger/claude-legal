# Florida Pro Se — Civil Framework

> **NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** Florida courts hold pro se litigants to
> the same procedural rules as attorneys. The Florida Courts
> Self-Help Center (https://www.flcourts.gov/Resources-Services/
> Court-Improvement/Court-Technology/Self-Represented-Litigants)
> has court-approved forms and guidance. Consider consulting a
> Florida Bar Lawyer Referral Service attorney for at least one
> consultation before proceeding.

## Florida pro se basics

- The **Florida Bar Lawyer Referral Service** connects litigants
  with attorneys for initial consultations:
  `https://www.floridabar.org/public/lrs/`
- **Florida Courts Self-Help Center** forms and resources:
  `https://www.flcourts.gov/Resources-Services/Court-Improvement/
  Court-Technology/Self-Represented-Litigants`
- Florida Courts have a **Self-Help Program** in many counties;
  lookup by county at the Florida Courts website

## PACE for pro se filers

Pro se litigants may file electronically through PACE:
`https://myflcourtaccess.flcourts.org`
- Registration is free
- Some courts still accept paper from pro se — verify at the
  clerk's office before filing

## Common Florida pro se mistakes

1. **Missing the caption closing line**: Florida captions end with
   a line of underscores and a forward slash `___/`
2. **Wrong court**: circuit court (over $50k) vs. county court
   (≤ $50k) vs. small claims (≤ $8k) — filing in the wrong
   division wastes time and money
3. **Missing the 20-day answer deadline**: Florida answer deadline
   is 20 days (Fla. R. Civ. P. 1.140(a)) — shorter than federal
4. **Not signing the document**: every pleading must be signed
   (Rule 1.030); unsigned documents can be stricken

## Service of process

Florida service is governed by Fla. Stat. § 48 (process service)
and Fla. R. Civ. P. 1.070 (summons). Personal service by the
**county sheriff** or a **certified process server** (§ 48.021).
Self-service is not permitted.
